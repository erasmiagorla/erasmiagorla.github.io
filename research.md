# Research

My work explores the presentation of the dead in theatre and cinema, with particular attention to the relationships among spectacle, ritual, affect, and dramatic form.

## Dissertation

### Necrospectacle: death, ritual, and representation

Why do tragedy and horror repeatedly render the dead visible when funerary rites seek to remove them from view?

My dissertation develops **necrospectacle** as a way of understanding theatrical and cinematic encounters with the dead. While funerary practices conceal or transform the corpse, stage and screen restore the dead to visibility. Dramatic and cinematic form can make this unsettling encounter available for sustained attention, even as narrative endings and the finite duration of performance attempt to recontain it.

The project places ancient Greek tragedy, early modern drama, Japanese Noh, and horror cinema in comparative conversation.
