# Theatre, performance, and the visible dead.

I study how tragedy and horror bring the dead into view—on stage and on screen—and what those encounters reveal about mortality, ritual, and spectatorship.

My doctoral dissertation develops the concept of **necrospectacle** to describe the theatrical and cinematic presentation of the dead as a supplementary death ritual. It examines how performance reopens the boundary between the living and the dead, and how aesthetic form attempts to contain what it exposes.

My research moves across ancient Greek tragedy, early modern drama, Japanese Noh theatre, and horror cinema.
